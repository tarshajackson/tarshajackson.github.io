About

I’m an accountant. For most of my professional life that has meant building financial systems, internal controls, and reporting for nonprofits in Omaha—community health centers, economic development organizations, and agencies whose work depends on reliable numbers.

Balance is still how I see the world. When the books balance, nothing is hidden and every entry is honored. Over time I noticed that some of the real value—trust, care, knowledge passed down, the simple fact of a person’s existence—never receives an entry. The official ledger stays closed to it.

That observation became The GEN Council and the Structural Conditions for Economic Participation framework. The framework is freely available, open to challenge, and begins from one premise: a person’s worth was never something that had to be earned. The question it opens is why taking part in economic life is still treated as something that must be.

I write the essays on Substack as the public continuation of that inquiry. They use the language I know—ledgers, recognition, going concern, agreement—to look at money, freedom, consent, and the difference between the number we write beside a person and the person who is actually there.
